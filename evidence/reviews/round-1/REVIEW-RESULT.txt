1. MAJOR — “Avoidable reminder” and its denominator are not operational enough

Location:
  PRAXIS.md, “Candidate failure stages,” line 24
  PRAXIS.md, “Definition of success,” line 44
  METHODOLOGY.md, “Question,” line 4
  METHODOLOGY.md, “Corpus protocol,” lines 15–19
  METHODOLOGY.md, “Baseline and evaluation,” lines 22–24
  ROADMAP.md, R0–R1, lines 9–10

Observed fact:
  A reminder counts when it supplies a capability or policy the agent “could reasonably have retrieved and used.” The proposed source set preferentially starts from incident, omission, and reminder records, while the primary outcome is the rate of avoidable omissions/reminders “among eligible cases.”

Reviewer judgment:
  “Reasonably,” “available,” “relevant,” and “correct use” lack a decision rule tied to what was accessible before the agent acted. A corpus assembled primarily from remembered incidents also lacks a denominator of capability opportunities or ordinary non-reminder tasks. It can estimate the composition of selected incidents, but not the rate at which reminders occur or improve.

Concrete consequence:
  A corpus containing mostly failures can make baseline failure prevalence appear high and make a tailored intervention look effective without demonstrating fewer reminders across eligible work.

Minimal correction:
  Before collecting cases, define:
  - the unit of analysis as a capability opportunity, not merely a reported reminder;
  - a bounded sampling frame and observation window;
  - inclusion of eligible non-reminder/control opportunities;
  - temporal availability and access at the original turn;
  - the pre-action evidence that should have triggered retrieval;
  - policy/permission eligibility and a reference expected action;
  - explicit exclusion of preference changes, new facts, hindsight-only knowledge, and ambiguous counterfactuals.

Disposition:
  REJECT.

Affected gate:
  Problem/acceptance fit; R0 and R1.

Verification required:
  Independent application of the codebook to examples and inversion cases, including at least one valid reminder, one new-information correction, one capability that existed but was not reasonably discoverable, and one correctly handled non-reminder task.
